# Design audit (R0.2)

Audited 2026-11-12 on a local production build (`wrangler dev`, local D1 with base + dev seeds and the e2e fixtures; all client
names are fictional). Before-screenshots of 29 screens at 390 and 1440: `docs/screenshots/redesign/before/` (webp, compressed). The
reusable capture harness is `playwright.visual.config.ts` + `tests/visual/shoot.spec.ts` (overflow + tap-target report per viewport).

## 1. Summary verdict

The site works: no horizontal overflow on any audited page, AA contrast is mostly met, semantics are sound. **Visually it is the
category default**: a dark hero with a decorative grid and a stock photo, then white sections of identical rounded cards with an icon
tile above a heading, the same "Learn more →" link in every card, and a dark CTA band before a dark footer. On phones every
desktop grid collapses into one long column of the same cards, so pages become very long scrolls with no rhythm:

| Page (390 px) | Page height | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Home | 10 251 px | 6 service cards + 6 "why" items + 5 steps + 4 photos + 5 values, all stacked |
| Pricing | 15 721 px | 24 packages as stacked cards, each repeating up to 18 feature rows |
| Web development division | 9 838 px | sub-service cards, packages, FAQ stacked |

`impeccable detect` (390 px): home flags **icon-tile-stack ×4** (the AI feature-card template), **codex-grid-background** (hero), a
**side-tab accent border** (`global.css` value list `border-l-2`), and a 3.8:1 placeholder in the footer newsletter input.

## 2. Weaknesses by area

**Header / nav.** Desktop: nine top-level items plus search, portal and CTA in one 64 px bar; dropdowns are plain lists. Mobile: a
hamburger in a bordered box opens a long list; no sense of place; the CTA disappears behind the menu. Nav buttons are 39 px tall.

**Hero (home + inner pages).** Grid-line background is decorative; the photo is a generic team shot; a floating "INV-2026 $750 paid"
chip is the only product hint. On phones the photo sits under the CTAs and adds 160 px of nothing. Inner pages repeat an eyebrow +
H1 + paragraph block on canvas grey with no identity.

**Cards and grids.** One card component everywhere (services, why, values, products, insights, packages). Icon tiles in 40 px rounded
squares above headings. Repetition makes nothing feel important.

**Services.** Six divisions rendered as six equal cards; sub-services are hidden one click deeper; nothing shows that packages and
prices exist until the pricing page.

**Pricing.** Accurate and complete, but: sticky category tabs are plain text links (36 px tall), packages stack as long cards on
phones with every feature row repeated (including "not included" rows), the comparison table is desktop-only and the recommended
package is only a 1 px outline + small badge. Add-ons are a plain list. No way to compare two packages on a phone.

**Our Work.** Empty state (correct, no fabricated projects) is a plain sentence in a box.

**Forms (contact, start-a-project, consultation).** Inputs 40 px tall with 15 px text (iOS zoom risk below 16 px), radio/checkbox
targets 13–20 px, labels above fields but no grouping or step feedback in the wizard.

**Footer.** Desktop four columns are fine; on phones it is a 900 px column of link lists, newsletter first, no contact channels
with icons, "Zimbabwe" as the only contact line when Settings are blank.

**Iconography.** Lucide is installed and used, but icons are decorative in tiles; contact channels, status, actions and nav have none.

**Motion.** Almost none: hover colour changes, an accordion; no reveal, no carousel, no tab indicator. Reduced motion is respected
because there is nothing to reduce.

**Client portal.** A top-nav web app (Overview, Projects, Quotations, Invoices, Payments, Services, Support, Account) on white; good
information (balance, quotations, projects, activity) but no workspace character, no status bar, no command palette, money in mono is
the only technical cue. Mobile: horizontal top nav scrolls off-screen.

**Admin.** Classic sidebar + top bar with KPI tiles and charts. Functional and dense; looks like the default admin template; 65 nav
items at 36 px; tables on phones become horizontally scrolled grids.

**Auth screens.** A centred card on grey for both portal and admin; nothing tells the two apart.

## 3. Pricing source of truth (BRIEF B.6)

- **Storage:** D1 tables `service_packages` (price_type, `price_minor` INTEGER + `currency`, `price_unit`, `annual_price_minor`,
  `min_quantity`, delivery_time, revision_rounds, support_period, `most_popular`, `pricing_tab`, status, `price_review_required`),
  `package_features` (per-package feature rows with included / value / not-included state), `package_addons`.
- **Origin:** seeded once by `seeds/base.sql` (generated by `scripts/seeds/build-base-seed.ts`), then **admin-managed** in
  Admin → Packages (`/admin/packages`, permission `packages.manage`; `packages.price_review` clears review flags).
- **Read path for the public site:** `getPricingCatalogue(ctx)` in `src/server/modules/cms/packages.ts` (respects the
  `pricing.show_prices` setting and package status), rendered by `src/pages/pricing.astro`, `PackageCard.astro`,
  `ComparisonTable.astro`, and the division pages (`/services/[slug]`). Price strings come from `formatPriceTag` in
  `src/shared/service-display.ts`; the comparison rows from `comparisonRows()` in the CMS module.
- **Rule for the redesign:** presentation only. The new pricing UI must keep reading the same catalogue functions; no copy of any
  price, feature or condition may be hard-coded. A new e2e test (R4.3) loads the catalogue from the local D1 and asserts every
  rendered package name, price tag, unit, feature value and add-on equals the stored data, at phone and desktop widths.
- Existing tests that pin pricing UI: `tests/e2e/pricing.spec.ts`, `packages.spec.ts` (selectors `[data-package]`, `[data-price]`,
  `[data-vat-note]`, "Pricing categories" navigation). The redesign keeps these hooks or updates the tests with a written reason.

## 5. Things that must survive the redesign
All routes and their HTTP behaviour; every form's fields, validation, Turnstile and honeypot; `data-*` test hooks used by e2e; JSON-LD;
CMS-driven sections and their visibility rules; "In Development" labelling of Business Suite; empty Our Work state; no registration
claims; the CSP (no inline scripts without hashes; fonts as files).
